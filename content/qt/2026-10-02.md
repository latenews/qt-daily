## Text (NIV)

**1** One day Samson went to Gaza, where he saw a prostitute. He went in to spend the night with her.
**2** The people of Gaza were told, "Samson is here!" So they surrounded the place and lay in wait for him all night at the city gate. They made no move during the night, saying, "At dawn we'll kill him."
**3** But Samson lay there only until the middle of the night. Then he got up and took hold of the doors of the city gate, together with the two posts, and tore them loose, bar and all. He lifted them to his shoulders and carried them to the top of the hill that faces Hebron.
**4** Some time later, he fell in love with a woman in the Valley of Sorek whose name was Delilah.
**5** The rulers of the Philistines went to her and said, "See if you can lure him into showing you the secret of his great strength and how we can overpower him so we may tie him up and subdue him. Each one of us will give you eleven hundred shekels of silver."
**6** So Delilah said to Samson, "Tell me the secret of your great strength and how you can be tied up and subdued."
**7** Samson answered her, "If anyone ties me with seven fresh thongs that have not been dried, I'll become as weak as any other man."
**8** Then the rulers of the Philistines brought her seven fresh thongs that had not been dried, and she tied him with them.
**9** With men hidden in the room, she called to him, "Samson, the Philistines are upon you!" But he snapped the thongs as easily as a piece of string snaps when it comes close to a flame. So the secret of his strength was not discovered.
**10** Then Delilah said to Samson, "You have made a fool of me; you lied to me. Come now, tell me how you can be tied."
**11** He said, "If anyone ties me securely with new ropes that have never been used, I'll become as weak as any other man."
**12** So Delilah took new ropes and tied him with them. Then, with men hidden in the room, she called to him, "Samson, the Philistines are upon you!" But he snapped the ropes off his arms as if they were threads.
**13** Delilah then said to Samson, "Until now, you have been making a fool of me and lying to me. Tell me how you can be tied." He replied, "If you weave the seven braids of my head into the fabric on the loom and tighten it with the pin, I'll become as weak as any other man." So while he was sleeping, Delilah took the seven braids of his head, wove them into the fabric
**14** and tightened it with the pin. Again she called to him, "Samson, the Philistines are upon you!" He awoke from his sleep and pulled up the pin and the loom, with the fabric.

## Observation

**Structure (2 parts)**
- Verses 1-3: Samson's brazen escape from Gaza after a night with a prostitute, showing his strength but also his recklessness
- Verses 4-14: The beginning of Delilah's calculated, repeated attempts to discover the secret of his strength

**Key repeated words/phrases**
- "the secret of your great strength" (v. 5, 6) — repeated as Delilah's explicit, stated goal from her very first approach to Samson, leaving no ambiguity about her intentions
- "Samson, the Philistines are upon you!" (v. 9, 12, 14) — repeated verbatim three times, forming an escalating test pattern that Samson repeatedly survives without apparent concern
- "as weak as any other man" (v. 7, 11, 13) — repeated in each of Samson's false answers, showing he understood exactly what was being sought and continued to toy with real danger

**Contrast structure**
- Samson's overwhelming physical feat (carrying off the entire city gate on his shoulders, v. 3) vs. his continued willingness to enter increasingly dangerous, deceptive games with Delilah — immense outward strength paired with poor discernment about who to trust
- Delilah's explicit, financially motivated collaboration with Samson's enemies (v. 5) vs. Samson's apparent obliviousness or indifference to this obvious threat — the narrative gives the reader clear knowledge that Samson does not seem to possess or act upon
- Three repeated tests, each ending in Samson's easy escape (vv. 9, 12, 14) vs. the escalating specificity of each answer, moving progressively closer to the true secret — a pattern of near-misses building tension toward an increasingly likely disaster

**Notable expressions**
- v. 3 carrying the city gate to a hilltop facing Hebron — an extraordinary display of strength used to mock and defy his enemies rather than for any covenantal or communal purpose
- v. 5 the specific sum, "eleven hundred shekels of silver," offered by each Philistine ruler — a large amount that reveals just how seriously and financially invested the Philistines were in eliminating Samson
- v. 13-14 Samson's answer moves from generic materials (thongs, ropes) to something intimately tied to his actual person (his hair woven into a loom) — the game is edging closer to real vulnerability with each round, though Samson still holds back the true secret

## Interpretation (Questions)

1. Why does Samson continue returning to dangerous, deceptive relationships (the prostitute in Gaza, then Delilah) despite his obvious strength and previous experiences of betrayal (as with his first wife)?
   → It reveals a persistent pattern of poor judgment in his personal life that his physical strength does nothing to correct; giftedness in one area does not guarantee wisdom or self-control in another, a theme that will prove fatal by the end of the chapter.

2. Why does Samson seem so unconcerned about Delilah's obvious, repeated attempts to discover his secret, even after being warned by the shout "the Philistines are upon you" three times?
   → It suggests either overconfidence in his own invincibility or a troubling blindness to real danger in a relationship he desires, showing how personal attachment or desire can dull discernment even in someone otherwise remarkably capable.

3. What does the escalating specificity of Samson's false answers (from ropes to his own hair) suggest about the direction this relationship and this chapter are heading?
   → It builds mounting tension and foreshadows Samson's eventual full disclosure — each answer edges closer to his actual vulnerability, showing a dangerous willingness to keep playing with the boundary of his own safety rather than removing himself from the threat altogether.

4. Why might the narrative include such specific, almost formulaic repetition (the same warning shouted three times) rather than simply summarizing that Delilah made several attempts?
   → The repetition heightens the reader's growing unease and creates dramatic suspense, letting the audience feel the mounting danger even while Samson himself remains apparently unbothered.

5. What warning does this passage offer about the relationship between remarkable gifts or strengths and genuine wisdom or discernment in personal relationships?
   → It shows that being remarkably gifted or strong in one domain provides no automatic protection against being deceived or manipulated in another, particularly in matters of the heart — discernment must be actively cultivated, not assumed from strength or success elsewhere.

## Application

- Examine whether there are relationships or patterns in my own life where I continue returning despite clear warning signs, perhaps assuming my own strength or capability in other areas will protect me here as well.
- Do not assume that success, gifting, or strength in one area of life automatically grants me wisdom or safety in another, particularly in close personal relationships.
- Pay attention to escalating patterns of compromise in my own life — small steps that edge closer to real vulnerability — before they reach a point of no return.
- Take repeated warnings seriously, even if I have previously escaped their consequences without harm; repeated safety in the past does not guarantee safety in the future.
