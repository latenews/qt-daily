## Text (NIV)

**25** That same night the LORD said to him, "Take the second bull from your father's herd, the one seven years old. Tear down your father's altar to Baal and cut down the Asherah pole beside it.
**26** Then build a proper kind of altar to the LORD your God on the top of this height. Using the wood of the Asherah pole that you cut down, offer the second bull as a burnt offering."
**27** So Gideon took ten of his servants and did as the LORD told him. But because he was afraid of his family and the men of the town, he did it at night rather than in the daytime.
**28** In the morning when the men of the town got up, there was Baal's altar, demolished, with the Asherah pole beside it cut down and the second bull sacrificed on the newly built altar!
**29** They asked each other, "Who did this?" When they carefully investigated, they were told, "Gideon son of Joash did it."
**30** The men of the town demanded of Joash, "Bring out your son. He must die, because he has broken down Baal's altar and cut down the Asherah pole beside it."
**31** But Joash replied to the hostile crowd around him, "Are you going to plead Baal's cause? Are you trying to save him? Whoever fights for him shall be put to death by morning! If Baal really is a god, he can defend himself when someone breaks down his altar."
**32** So that day they called Gideon "Jerub-Baal," saying, "Let Baal contend with him," because he broke down Baal's altar.
**33** Now all the Midianites, Amalekites and other eastern peoples joined forces and crossed over the Jordan and camped in the Valley of Jezreel.
**34** Then the Spirit of the LORD came upon Gideon, and he blew a trumpet, summoning the Abiezrites to follow him.
**35** He sent messengers throughout Manasseh, calling them to arms, and also into Asher, Zebulun and Naphtali, so that they too went up to meet them.
**36** Gideon said to God, "If you will save Israel by my hand as you have promised-
**37** look, I will place a wool fleece on the threshing floor. If there is dew only on the fleece and all the ground is dry, then I will know that you will save Israel by my hand, as you said."
**38** And that is what happened. Gideon rose early the next day; he squeezed the fleece and wrung out the dew-a bowlful of water.
**39** Then Gideon said to God, "Do not be angry with me. Let me make just one more request. Allow me one more test with the fleece. This time make the fleece dry and the ground covered with dew."
**40** That night God did so. Only the fleece was dry; all the ground was covered with dew.

## Observation

**Structure (3 parts)**
- Verses 25-27: The LORD's first specific assignment for Gideon — tearing down his own father's Baal altar — done in fear, at night
- Verses 28-32: The town's furious reaction and Joash's surprising defense of his son, resulting in Gideon's new name
- Verses 33-40: The gathering of an army and Gideon's two fleece tests before committing to battle

**Key repeated words/phrases**
- "afraid" (v. 27) — named plainly as Gideon's motive for acting at night, showing his obedience and his fear coexisting rather than one canceling the other
- "Let Baal contend with him" (v. 32) — the mocking nickname "Jerub-Baal" becomes, ironically, a badge tied to Gideon's identity throughout the rest of the story
- "one more" (v. 39, twice implied through the repeated fleece test) — Gideon's need for reassurance is not satisfied by a single sign

**Contrast structure**
- Gideon's private, fearful, nighttime obedience (v. 27) vs. the LORD's Spirit publicly and boldly coming upon him to summon an army (v. 34) — the same man moves from hidden fear to public leadership within a few verses
- Joash's likely expected reaction (siding with the angry crowd against his own son) vs. his actual sharp, faith-filled defense ("if Baal really is a god, he can defend himself," v. 31) — a father's unexpected theological clarity turns danger into vindication
- Gideon asking God twice for a physical sign (dew on fleece, then dry fleece) even after already being filled with the Spirit and gathering an army (v. 34-40) — outward empowerment does not automatically remove inward need for reassurance

**Notable expressions**
- v. 27 "did it at night rather than in the daytime" — the text records his fear without excusing it or hiding it, modeling an unflinching honesty about mixed motives in obedience
- v. 31 "If Baal really is a god, he can defend himself" — Joash's logic quietly and effectively exposes the emptiness of idolatry using the town's own religious assumptions
- v. 39 "Do not be angry with me" — Gideon is aware that repeated requests for confirmation could reasonably provoke God's anger, yet he asks anyway, and the LORD graciously grants it without rebuke

## Interpretation (Questions)

1. Why does the LORD's first specific instruction to Gideon involve tearing down his own father's household altar rather than immediately confronting the Midianite army?
   → Before Gideon can lead Israel in a public battle against external oppression, he must first deal decisively with idolatry close to home; deliverance begins with personal and household reformation, not only with external victory.

2. Why does the text openly state that Gideon acted "at night" because he "was afraid," rather than presenting him as fearless?
   → It offers an honest picture of obedience under fear — Gideon obeys fully even though imperfectly and fearfully, showing that the LORD can use obedience that is real but not yet courageous, without waiting for fear to disappear first.

3. Why is Joash's response to the mob (v. 31) so significant, and what does it reveal about the nature of true versus false gods?
   → Joash's simple logic — that a real god should be able to defend his own altar — exposes idolatry's fundamental weakness. It also shows that sometimes the sharpest, most freeing theological insight comes unexpectedly from someone we might not expect, even a father protecting his son.

4. Why does Gideon ask for not just one but two fleece tests, especially after already having received the Spirit and rallying an army?
   → It shows that even outward signs of calling and empowerment (the Spirit's presence, an assembled army) do not automatically eliminate the human need for reassurance before a costly, dangerous commitment; God's patience with Gideon's repeated requests reflects grace toward genuine, anxious faith rather than impatience with it.

5. What does God's willingness to grant both fleece requests, without becoming angry as Gideon feared, teach about how God responds to hesitant but sincere faith?
   → It reveals a God who is patient with imperfect faith seeking confirmation, distinguishing this from testing God out of unbelief or manipulation; God meets Gideon exactly where his weakness is, without shaming him for needing reassurance.

## Application

- Consider what "household altar" — a private compromise or idolatry close to home — might need to be torn down in my own life before I can be used more publicly for God's purposes.
- Recognize that obedience which is fearful, hesitant, or done quietly at night is still real obedience; I do not need to wait until fear disappears before acting rightly.
- Look for the unexpected sources of clear, faith-filled wisdom in my life — sometimes it comes from people I would not expect, as it did through Joash.
- When I need reassurance before a costly step of obedience, bring that request to God honestly, trusting his patience rather than assuming he will be angry with me for asking.
