## Text (NIV)

**15** Then she said to him, "How can you say, 'I love you,' when you won't confide in me? This is the third time you have made a fool of me and haven't told me the secret of your great strength."
**16** With such nagging she prodded him day after day until he was tired to death.
**17** So he told her everything. "No razor has ever been used on my head," he said, "because I have been a Nazirite set apart to God since birth. If my head were shaved, my strength would leave me, and I would become as weak as any other man."
**18** When Delilah saw that he had told her everything, she sent word to the rulers of the Philistines, "Come back once more; he has told me everything." So the rulers of the Philistines returned with the silver in their hands.
**19** Having put him to sleep on her lap, she called a man to shave off the seven braids of his hair, and so began to subdue him. And his strength left him.
**20** Then she called, "Samson, the Philistines are upon you!" He awoke from his sleep and thought, "I'll go out as before and shake myself free." But he did not know that the LORD had left him.
**21** Then the Philistines seized him, gouged out his eyes and took him down to Gaza. Binding him with bronze shackles, they set him to grinding in the prison.
**22** But the hair on his head began to grow again after it had been shaved.
**23** Now the rulers of the Philistines assembled to offer a great sacrifice to Dagon their god and to celebrate, saying, "Our god has delivered Samson, our enemy, into our hands."
**24** When the people saw him, they praised their god, saying, "Our god has delivered our enemy into our hands, the one who laid waste our land and multiplied our slain."
**25** While they were in high spirits, they shouted, "Bring out Samson to entertain us." So they called Samson out of the prison, and he performed for them. When they stood him among the pillars,
**26** Samson said to the servant who held his hand, "Put me where I can feel the pillars that support the temple, so that I may lean against them."
**27** Now the temple was crowded with men and women; all the rulers of the Philistines were there, and on the roof were about three thousand men and women watching Samson perform.
**28** Then Samson prayed to the LORD, "O Sovereign LORD, remember me. O God, please strengthen me just once more, and let me with one blow get revenge on the Philistines for my two eyes."
**29** Then Samson reached toward the two central pillars on which the temple stood. Bracing himself against them, his right hand on the one and his left hand on the other,
**30** Samson said, "Let me die with the Philistines!" Then he pushed with all his might, and down came the temple on the rulers and all the people in it. Thus he killed many more when he died than while he lived.
**31** Then his brothers and his father's whole family went down to get him. They brought him back and buried him between Zorah and Eshtaol in the tomb of Manoah his father. He had led Israel twenty years.

## Observation

**Structure (3 parts)**
- Verses 15-22: Delilah's final, successful manipulation, the loss of Samson's strength, and his capture and blinding
- Verses 23-27: The Philistines' celebration and Samson's public humiliation as entertainment
- Verses 28-31: Samson's final prayer, his last act of strength, and his death and burial

**Key repeated words/phrases**
- "he did not know" (v. 20) — the single most devastating phrase in the chapter, marking the moment Samson's confidence outlasted the actual source of his power
- "delivered... into our hands" (v. 23, 24) — repeated in the Philistines' celebratory worship, crediting Dagon for what was, in fact, a consequence of Samson's own disclosure and disobedience
- "once more" (v. 28) — Samson's final prayer asks for strength "just once more," acknowledging this is a final, not habitual, request rather than a return to his former unreflective confidence

**Contrast structure**
- Samson's assumption, "I'll go out as before and shake myself free" (v. 20), vs. the narrator's immediate correction, "he did not know that the LORD had left him" — confidence built on past experience collapses the moment its true foundation is gone
- The Philistines' loud, public praise of Dagon for Samson's capture (vv. 23-24) vs. the quiet, private truth that it was Samson's own disclosure and the LORD's withdrawal, not Dagon's power, that brought him down — the narrative lets the reader see the theological error the Philistines themselves cannot
- Samson's earlier prayer motivated by personal revenge ("let me... get revenge on the Philistines for my two eyes," v. 28) vs. the far greater, national deliverance actually accomplished through his death (v. 30) — mixed motives are once again used by God for purposes larger than Samson's own stated intent
- "he killed many more when he died than while he lived" (v. 30) — Samson's ultimate impact comes not from a lifetime of strength but from a single moment of surrendered, sacrificial death

**Notable expressions**
- v. 20 "he did not know that the LORD had left him" — perhaps the most tragic line in the entire book of Judges, capturing how easily strength can be presumed upon once its true source has quietly departed
- v. 22 "the hair... began to grow again" — a small, quiet detail planted well before the climax, subtly signaling that restoration, though costly and incomplete, is still possible even after catastrophic failure
- v. 28 "remember me" — Samson's final prayer is strikingly humble compared to his earlier boasting, showing a measure of genuine, if late, dependence on God rather than his own strength

## Interpretation (Questions)

1. Why does the narrator include the devastating aside "he did not know that the LORD had left him" (v. 20) rather than simply narrating that Samson's strength failed?
   → It pinpoints the precise theological tragedy of the scene — Samson's confidence had shifted from genuine dependence on the LORD to presumption upon his own past experience, and the gap between the two proved fatal; strength without ongoing awareness of its source becomes a dangerous illusion.

2. What is the significance of the small detail that Samson's hair "began to grow again" (v. 22), placed quietly before the story's climax?
   → It signals that God's grace toward Samson had not entirely ended even after such serious failure and disobedience — restoration remained possible, though it would come at great, sacrificial cost rather than a simple return to his former life.

3. Why does Samson's final prayer (v. 28) ask God to "remember" him and to grant strength "just once more," rather than his earlier pattern of simply acting on assumed strength?
   → It reflects a genuinely humbled posture, quite different from his former overconfidence — Samson now explicitly acknowledges his complete dependence on God's action rather than his own inherent ability, even while his motive remains partly personal revenge.

4. How does the contrast between the Philistines' loud public praise of Dagon (vv. 23-24) and the narrator's quiet theological correction shape how the reader should interpret events that appear, on the surface, to favor the "wrong side"?
   → It teaches the reader not to interpret apparent success or celebration as proof of ultimate truth or victory; the Philistines' triumphant worship is shown to rest on a false, temporary understanding that the story's ending will completely overturn.

5. What does it mean that Samson "killed many more when he died than while he lived" (v. 30), and how does this shape the overall assessment of his troubled, flawed life?
   → It suggests that God's purposes were accomplished not through Samson's lifetime of impressive but self-serving strength, but through a final act of humbled, sacrificial surrender — even a deeply flawed life can conclude with a moment of genuine, costly faithfulness that God uses for significant purpose.

## Application

- Examine whether my own confidence in areas of past strength or success has quietly outlasted genuine, present dependence on God; ask him to reveal any place where I am presuming on strength that may no longer be sustained by real closeness to him.
- Take comfort that even after serious failure or disobedience, restoration remains possible with God, though it may come at real cost rather than an easy return to how things were before.
- Practice praying with the humility of Samson's final prayer — genuinely acknowledging dependence on God's action rather than assuming my own capability — especially in moments that matter most.
- Resist interpreting apparent worldly success or celebration as proof of ultimate truth, remembering that appearances of victory can be temporary and built on false understanding.
