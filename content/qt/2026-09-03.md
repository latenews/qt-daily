## Text (NIV)

**22** Now the house of Joseph attacked Bethel, and the LORD was with them.
**23** When they sent men to spy out Bethel (formerly called Luz),
**24** the spies saw a man coming out of the city and they said to him, "Show us how to get into the city and we will see that you are treated well."
**25** So he showed them, and they put the city to the sword but spared the man and his whole family.
**26** He then went to the land of the Hittites, where he built a city and called it Luz, which is its name to this day.
**27** But Manasseh did not drive out the people of Beth Shan or Taanach or Dor or Ibleam or Megiddo and their surrounding settlements, for the Canaanites were determined to live in that land.
**28** When Israel became strong, they pressed the Canaanites into forced labor but never drove them out completely.
**29** Nor did Ephraim drive out the Canaanites living in Gezer, but the Canaanites continued to live there among them.
**30** Neither did Zebulun drive out the Canaanites living in Kitron or Nahalol, who remained among them; but they did subject them to forced labor.
**31** Nor did Asher drive out those living in Acco or Sidon or Ahlab or Aczib or Helbah or Aphek or Rehob,
**32** and because of this the people of Asher lived among the Canaanite inhabitants of the land.
**33** Neither did Naphtali drive out those living in Beth Shemesh or Beth Anath; but the Naphtalites too lived among the Canaanite inhabitants of the land, and those living in Beth Shemesh and Beth Anath became forced laborers for them.
**34** The Amorites confined the Danites to the hill country, not allowing them to come down into the plain.
**35** And the Amorites were determined also to hold out in Mount Heres, Aijalon and Shaalbim, but when the power of the house of Joseph increased, they too were pressed into forced labor.
**36** The boundary of the Amorites was from Scorpion Pass to Sela and beyond.

## Observation

**Structure (2 parts)**
- Verses 22-26: The house of Joseph's capture of Bethel through a spared informant, who then rebuilds a city elsewhere
- Verses 27-36: An escalating catalog of six tribes (Manasseh, Ephraim, Zebulun, Asher, Naphtali, Dan) failing to fully drive out the Canaanites

**Key repeated words/phrases**
- "did not drive out" / "nor did... drive out" / "neither did... drive out" (v. 27, 29, 30, 31, 33) — repeated with only slight variation across five consecutive tribes, forming a drumbeat of accumulating failure
- "forced labor" (v. 28, 30, 33, 35) — repeated as the default compromise: rather than obedience (driving out) or destruction, Israel repeatedly settles for economic subjugation instead
- "lived among" (v. 32, 33) — used to describe the resulting mixed communities, foreshadowing the religious syncretism that chapter 2 will identify as the direct cause of Israel's downfall

**Contrast structure**
- Bethel's capture through mercy shown to a spared informant (vv. 24-25) vs. the six subsequent tribes' compromises through half-measures and forced labor — one act of shown kindness (though morally ambiguous, since it enabled a city's fall) stands apart from the pattern of settling for less than full obedience
- "the LORD was with them" at Bethel (v. 22) vs. no such statement accompanying any of the following failures — the narrative's silence about the LORD's presence in the following six accounts is conspicuous by omission
- "when Israel became strong" (v. 28) and "when the power of the house of Joseph increased" (v. 35) vs. their choice to use that strength for forced labor rather than complete obedience — growing strength is used to manage compromise rather than to finish the task

**Notable expressions**
- v. 26 "he... built a city and called it Luz" — the spared man does not simply disappear but goes on to found a new settlement elsewhere, a small, almost throwaway detail suggesting that incomplete conquest has ripple effects extending beyond Israel's own borders
- v. 27 "for the Canaanites were determined to live in that land" — this explanation shifts attention to the Canaanites' resolve rather than Israel's own effort, subtly softening what will later be revealed (2:1-3) as fundamentally Israel's own failure of obedience
- v. 28-35 the list format itself — six tribes named one after another with almost identical language — mimics a legal or administrative record, allowing the sheer repetition to make the point more forcefully than any single statement could

## Interpretation (Questions)

1. Why does the chapter move from a specific, narrated victory at Bethel (vv. 22-26) into an extended, repetitive list of failures across six different tribes (vv. 27-36)?
   → The literary shift from narrative to list format itself makes a point: individual, specific victories (like Bethel) increasingly give way to a pattern so widespread and repetitive across the nation that it can only be represented by a catalog, foreshadowing the systemic nature of Israel's coming spiritual crisis.

2. What is the significance of "forced labor" becoming the repeated substitute for complete obedience across multiple tribes (vv. 28, 30, 33, 35)?
   → It reveals a pattern of settling for practical, profitable compromise rather than full obedience to God's command — subjugating rather than removing the Canaanites may have seemed like a reasonable, even successful, outcome by human economic standards, while still constituting disobedience to what God had actually commanded.

3. Why might the text note that growing Israelite strength ("when Israel became strong," v. 28; "when the power of the house of Joseph increased," v. 35) is used to manage ongoing compromise rather than to finally complete the conquest?
   → It shows that increased strength or resources do not automatically lead to greater faithfulness; without a renewed commitment to full obedience, growing power can simply be used to make an already-compromised situation more comfortable or sustainable rather than to correct it.

4. What does the phrase "the Canaanites were determined to live in that land" (v. 27) suggest about how the text frames responsibility for these failures at this point in the narrative, compared to what chapter 2 will reveal?
   → At this stage, the text seems to allow the Canaanites' own resolve to share the explanatory weight for Israel's incomplete conquest; chapter 2 will sharpen and correct this framing, revealing that ultimate responsibility lies with Israel's own failure to fully trust and obey the LORD.

5. Why does the detail about the spared man from Bethel founding a new city called Luz (v. 26) matter within this larger account of partial conquest?
   → It is a small reminder that incomplete or compromised obedience does not simply disappear without consequence — even an act that seemed to secure victory (sparing an informant) results in the continuation and spread of Canaanite culture and identity elsewhere, subtly reinforcing the larger point of the chapter.

## Application

- Examine whether I have settled for a manageable compromise — a kind of "forced labor" arrangement with something that should have been fully removed from my life — rather than pursuing complete obedience because the compromise seemed practical or even beneficial.
- Consider whether increased strength, resources, or success in my own life are being used to pursue deeper faithfulness or simply to make ongoing compromise more comfortable and sustainable.
- Resist the temptation to explain away my own incomplete obedience primarily by pointing to external resistance or difficulty, and instead honestly examine my own will and effort in the matter.
- Recognize that a compromise which currently seems settled and manageable may have longer-term consequences and ripple effects that are not yet visible.

## Prayer

Lord, show me anywhere I have settled for a manageable, even profitable, compromise instead of pursuing the complete obedience you have actually called me to. As you give me strength or success, let me use it to pursue deeper faithfulness rather than to make an existing compromise more comfortable. Keep me from explaining away my own incomplete obedience by pointing only to external difficulty, and give me honest eyes to see my own will in the matter. And help me trust that no compromise is as small or contained as it seems, since even quiet accommodations can carry consequences far beyond what I can presently see. Amen.
