## Text (NIV)

**1** After the death of Joshua, the Israelites asked the LORD, "Who will be the first to go up and fight for us against the Canaanites?"
**2** The LORD answered, "Judah is to go; I have given the land into their hands."
**3** Then the men of Judah said to the Simeonites their brothers, "Come up with us into the territory allotted to us, to fight against the Canaanites. We in turn will go with you into yours." So the Simeonites went with them.
**4** When Judah attacked, the LORD gave the Canaanites and Perizzites into their hands and they struck down ten thousand men at Bezek.
**5** It was there that they found Adoni-Bezek and fought against him, putting to rout the Canaanites and Perizzites.
**6** Adoni-Bezek fled, but they chased him and caught him, and cut off his thumbs and big toes.
**7** Then Adoni-Bezek said, "Seventy kings with their thumbs and big toes cut off have picked up scraps under my table. Now God has paid me back for what I did to them." They brought him to Jerusalem, and he died there.
**8** The men of Judah attacked Jerusalem also and took it. They put the city to the sword and set it on fire.
**9** After that, the men of Judah went down to fight against the Canaanites living in the hill country, the Negev and the western foothills.
**10** They advanced against the Canaanites living in Hebron (formerly called Kiriath Arba) and defeated Sheshai, Ahiman and Talmai.

## Observation

**Structure (2 parts)**
- Verses 1-3: Israel inquires of the LORD after Joshua's death, and Judah is named to lead, forming an alliance with Simeon
- Verses 4-10: The first campaigns against the Canaanites, culminating in Adoni-Bezek's capture and confession, and the taking of Jerusalem and Hebron

**Key repeated words/phrases**
- "asked the LORD" (v. 1) — the book opens, notably, with Israel seeking direction rather than acting on its own initiative, setting a positive tone before the later drift of the book
- "gave... into their hands" (v. 4) — the familiar language of divine gift frames the victory as the LORD's doing even as Judah does the fighting
- "cut off" (v. 6, echoed in Adoni-Bezek's own words in v. 7) — the same mutilation both inflicted and confessed as deserved, tying cause and consequence together explicitly

**Contrast structure**
- Israel's opening posture of asking the LORD for direction (v. 1) vs. the later chapters' pattern of doing evil without asking anything — this early scene stands as a hopeful contrast to what the book will increasingly document
- Adoni-Bezek's cruelty toward "seventy kings" (v. 7) vs. his own identical mutilation at Judah's hands — a clear, self-acknowledged case of just retribution, rare in its explicitness
- Judah's cooperative alliance with Simeon ("we in turn will go with you into yours," v. 3) vs. the later internal conflicts among tribes seen elsewhere in Judges (e.g., Ephraim and Gilead) — early unity contrasts with later division

**Notable expressions**
- v. 1 "who will be the first to go up" — a question of leadership and initiative asked directly to the LORD, showing appropriate dependence at the outset of this new phase after Joshua's death
- v. 7 "Now God has paid me back for what I did to them" — a striking moment where an enemy king himself recognizes and names divine justice in his own downfall, without prompting from Israel
- v. 6 the specific, brutal detail of cutting off thumbs and big toes — a practical, humiliating method of ensuring a captured king could never again wield a weapon or lead an army, common in the ancient Near East

## Interpretation (Questions)

1. Why does the book of Judges open with Israel asking the LORD for direction (v. 1), when so much of the book that follows documents Israel's drift away from seeking him?
   → It establishes a baseline of appropriate dependence on God at a moment of leadership transition, making the later, repeated departures from this pattern all the more striking and tragic by contrast.

2. Why is Adoni-Bezek's own acknowledgment of divine justice (v. 7) included in the narrative, rather than the text simply reporting his capture and death?
   → It shows that even a pagan king outside the covenant community can recognize the moral logic of just retribution, reinforcing the narrative's implicit claim that God's justice operates in ways even his enemies can perceive and confess.

3. What does the cooperative alliance between Judah and Simeon (v. 3) suggest about how the conquest was intended to proceed, in contrast to how later chapters will show it unraveling?
   → It presents a model of tribal cooperation and mutual support in pursuing God's purposes, setting up a positive counter-example to the internal Israelite conflicts (like Ephraim versus Gilead) that emerge later in the book.

4. Why does the narrative pair spiritual dependence ("asked the LORD," v. 1) directly with military action (vv. 4-10), rather than separating prayer from practical effort?
   → It models a pattern where seeking God's direction and taking decisive, courageous action are not opposed but work together — dependence on God does not mean passivity, but proceeds into faithful effort once direction is given.

5. What might the early success described in this opening section (Bezek, Jerusalem, Hebron) suggest about what is possible when Israel begins in a posture of inquiry and cooperation?
   → It suggests that unity and genuine seeking of the LORD's guidance produce real, tangible success — a pattern the rest of Judges will show being lost as Israel drifts from both practices simultaneously.

## Application

- Begin significant undertakings, transitions, or decisions by genuinely asking God for direction, as Israel did at the start of this new period, rather than assuming I already know the way forward.
- Look for opportunities for cooperation and mutual support with others pursuing the same faithful purposes, following the example of Judah and Simeon rather than working in isolation or competition.
- Remember that dependence on God and decisive, courageous action belong together; seeking his guidance is not a substitute for faithful effort once that guidance is given.
- Take note of what genuine unity and God-seeking produce compared to what division and self-reliance produce later in this same book, and let that shape how seriously I take both practices in my own life.

## Prayer

Lord, at the start of any new season or challenge, teach me to ask you for direction first, the way Israel did here, rather than assuming I already know the way forward on my own. Give me a spirit of cooperation with others who are pursuing your purposes, rather than isolation or rivalry. Help me to hold together genuine dependence on you with decisive, faithful action, not treating either one as a substitute for the other. And let the contrast between this hopeful beginning and the drift that follows in this book warn me to guard my own heart against the same slow departure from seeking you. Amen.
