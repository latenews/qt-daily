## Text (NIV)

**1** Keep me safe, O God, for in you I take refuge.
**2** I said to the LORD, "You are my Lord; apart from you I have no good thing."
**3** As for the saints who are in the land, they are the glorious ones in whom is all my delight.
**4** The sorrows of those will increase who run after other gods. I will not pour out their libations of blood or take up their names on my lips.
**5** LORD, you have assigned me my portion and my cup; you have made my lot secure.
**6** The boundary lines have fallen for me in pleasant places; surely I have a delightful inheritance.
**7** I will praise the LORD, who counsels me; even at night my heart instructs me.
**8** I have set the LORD always before me. Because he is at my right hand, I will not be shaken.
**9** Therefore my heart is glad and my tongue rejoices; my body also will rest secure,
**10** because you will not abandon me to the grave, nor will you let your Holy One see decay.
**11** You have made known to me the path of life; you will fill me with joy in your presence, with eternal pleasures at your right hand.

## Observation

**Structure (3 parts)**
- Verses 1-4: An opening declaration of refuge in God alone, delight in fellow believers, and rejection of other gods
- Verses 5-8: Confidence in God's provision and guidance, described through the imagery of inheritance and unwavering presence
- Verses 9-11: A confident conclusion moving from present gladness to hope beyond death itself

**Key repeated words/phrases**
- "portion" / "lot" / "inheritance" (v. 5, 6) — repeated land-allotment imagery describing God himself as the psalmist's true possession and security
- "LORD... at my right hand" (v. 8) — echoed again in the psalm's final line, "eternal pleasures at your right hand" (v. 11), bookending the psalm with this image of intimate divine nearness
- "will not" (v. 4, 8, 10) — a series of confident negative statements (will not pour out libations, will not be shaken, will not abandon) building a cumulative sense of settled security

**Contrast structure**
- Those who "run after other gods" and whose "sorrows will increase" (v. 4) vs. the psalmist's own settled contentment, "apart from you I have no good thing" (v. 2) — divided, restless devotion is contrasted with singular, satisfied trust
- "The boundary lines have fallen for me in pleasant places" (v. 6) vs. the ordinary human experience of comparing one's portion or lot unfavorably to others' — the psalmist expresses full contentment with what God has specifically assigned
- Being "abandon[ed]... to the grave" and seeing "decay" (v. 10) vs. being shown "the path of life" and filled with "eternal pleasures" (v. 11) — death's apparent finality is directly confronted and overturned by confident hope

**Notable expressions**
- v. 3 "the saints who are in the land, they are the glorious ones in whom is all my delight" — an unusual but striking statement locating deep personal delight not only in God directly but also in fellow believers, showing that genuine devotion to God naturally includes delight in God's people
- v. 7 "even at night my heart instructs me" — suggests that spiritual formation and guidance continue even in quiet, unconscious hours, not only through deliberate daytime reflection
- v. 10 "you will not abandon me to the grave, nor will you let your Holy One see decay" — this verse is directly applied to Christ's resurrection in Acts 2:25-31 and 13:35, showing how this personal psalm of trust ultimately finds its fullest meaning in Jesus

## Interpretation (Questions)

1. Why does the psalmist begin with such an absolute statement of exclusive devotion — "apart from you I have no good thing" (v. 2) — rather than acknowledging various sources of good in life?
   → It establishes God not merely as one good thing among many but as the singular source and standard by which all other good things are measured and enjoyed; genuine contentment flows from this settled, exclusive orientation rather than from balancing multiple ultimate loyalties.

2. What is the significance of describing God himself, using inheritance and land-portion language, as "my portion and my cup" (v. 5) and the source of "pleasant places" (v. 6)?
   → It reframes contentment and security not in terms of material possession or circumstance but in terms of relationship with God himself; the psalmist's true wealth and inheritance is God's own presence and provision, not external circumstances that could change.

3. Why does the psalmist include delight in "the saints who are in the land" (v. 3) within a psalm primarily focused on personal trust in God?
   → It shows that authentic devotion to God is not a purely individualistic experience; genuine delight in God naturally extends to genuine delight in God's people, since shared devotion to the same Lord creates real spiritual community and mutual joy.

4. What does the confident claim "I will not be shaken" (v. 8), grounded in God being "at my right hand," suggest about the source of true stability?
   → It locates stability not in favorable circumstances or personal strength but specifically in continual, conscious awareness of God's presence; stability is relational and dependent on maintaining that awareness ("I have set the LORD always before me") rather than being an independent personal trait.

5. How does the New Testament's application of verse 10 to Christ's resurrection (Acts 2, 13) shape how this psalm should ultimately be read and understood?
   → It reveals that while David could express genuine personal hope in these words, their fullest and ultimate fulfillment is found in Jesus Christ, whose actual bodily resurrection secured exactly what this psalm anticipates — victory over death and decay — meaning believers today can read this psalm both as a model of personal trust and as pointing toward the risen Christ.

## Application

- Examine whether my sense of contentment and security is genuinely rooted in God himself as my ultimate "portion," or whether it depends more on favorable circumstances that could shift.
- Cultivate genuine delight not only in private devotion to God but also in fellowship with other believers, recognizing shared faith as a source of real joy rather than treating faith as purely individual.
- Practice deliberately "setting the LORD always before me" through specific habits of remembrance and attention throughout the day, recognizing that stability flows from sustained awareness of his presence.
- Let this psalm's confident hope beyond death, fulfilled in Christ's resurrection, shape how I face mortality and loss, trusting in the same victory over decay that God accomplished for Jesus.

## Prayer

Lord, you are my portion; apart from you I have no lasting good. Teach me contentment rooted in your presence rather than in circumstances that can change. Give me genuine delight in your people, not only in private devotion to you alone. Help me to set you before me deliberately throughout each day, so that my stability comes from sustained awareness of your nearness. And thank you that in Christ's resurrection, the hope this psalm anticipates has been fully secured — you have shown me, and shown the world, the path of life. Amen.
