## Text (NIV)

**1** Hear, O LORD, my righteous plea; listen to my cry. Give ear to my prayer-it does not rise from deceitful lips.
**2** May my vindication come from you; may your eyes see what is right.
**3** Though you probe my heart and examine me at night, though you test me, you will find nothing; I have resolved that my mouth will not sin.
**4** As for the deeds of men-by the word of your lips I have kept myself from the ways of the violent.
**5** My steps have held to your paths; my feet have not slipped.
**6** I call on you, O God, for you will answer me; give ear to me and hear my prayer.
**7** Show the wonder of your great love, you who save by your right hand those who take refuge in you from their foes.
**8** Keep me as the apple of your eye; hide me in the shadow of your wings
**9** from the wicked who assail me, from my mortal enemies who surround me.
**10** They close up their callous hearts, and their mouths speak with arrogance.
**11** They have tracked me down, they now surround me, with eyes alert, to throw me to the ground.
**12** They are like a lion hungry for prey, like a great lion crouching in cover.
**13** Rise up, O LORD, confront them, bring them down; rescue me from the wicked by your sword.
**14** O LORD, by your hand save me from such men, from men of this world whose reward is in this life. You still the hunger of those you cherish; their sons have plenty, and they store up wealth for their children.
**15** And I-in righteousness I will see your face; when I awake, I will be satisfied with seeing your likeness.

## Observation

**Structure (3 parts)**
- Verses 1-5: An appeal to God based on the psalmist's examined integrity and righteous conduct
- Verses 6-12: An urgent cry for protection, using vivid imagery of predatory enemies
- Verses 13-15: A direct plea for deliverance, contrasted with the fleeting reward of worldly men, ending in confident hope of seeing God's face

**Key repeated words/phrases**
- "hear... give ear... hear my prayer" (v. 1, 6) — repeated at the start of both major sections, framing the whole psalm as an urgent, direct appeal for God's attention
- "eyes" (v. 2, "your eyes see what is right"; v. 8, "apple of your eye") — used both of God's discerning judgment and his intimate, protective care
- "hand" (v. 7, "save by your right hand"; v. 14, "by your hand save me") — repeated to emphasize that deliverance comes through God's own direct action

**Contrast structure**
- The psalmist's examined, tested integrity ("you will find nothing," v. 3) vs. the enemies' "callous hearts" and "arrogance" (v. 10) — two starkly different inner conditions set against each other
- Enemies "like a lion hungry for prey" (v. 12), actively hunting and surrounding vs. the psalmist hidden "in the shadow of your wings" (v. 8) — predatory aggression contrasted with intimate divine shelter
- "Men of this world whose reward is in this life," who "store up wealth for their children" (v. 14) vs. the psalmist's own anticipated reward: seeing God's "face" and being "satisfied with seeing your likeness" (v. 15) — temporal material accumulation is set against eternal relational satisfaction
- Falling asleep in danger vs. waking to see God — "when I awake, I will be satisfied" (v. 15) suggests confidence that endures through the vulnerability of sleep itself

**Notable expressions**
- v. 3 "though you probe my heart and examine me at night... you will find nothing" — a bold claim of examined integrity, grounded not in self-deception but in genuine confidence before God's own thorough scrutiny
- v. 8 "keep me as the apple of your eye; hide me in the shadow of your wings" — combines two intimate images, one emphasizing precious value (the eye's most sensitive, protected part) and one emphasizing sheltering protection (a bird covering its young)
- v. 15 "when I awake, I will be satisfied with seeing your likeness" — a phrase traditionally understood both as immediate hope (waking each morning in God's presence) and ultimate hope (waking to resurrection and the direct sight of God)

## Interpretation (Questions)

1. Why does the psalmist open with such a bold claim of examined integrity (vv. 1-5), inviting God's own thorough scrutiny ("probe my heart... examine me at night") rather than a more generalized appeal for mercy?
   → It reflects genuine confidence that his prayer is not self-serving or hypocritical; rather than merely asking for help while ignoring his own conduct, he grounds his appeal in a life that has been deliberately and carefully kept from sin, inviting God's own confirming judgment.

2. What is the significance of combining the images of "the apple of your eye" and "the shadow of your wings" (v. 8) to describe the kind of protection being requested?
   → Together they express both intense value (something precious and carefully guarded) and close, sheltering intimacy (young covered protectively under a parent's wings), showing that the psalmist seeks not merely rescue from danger but the security of being genuinely cherished by God.

3. Why does the psalmist describe his enemies with such vivid predatory imagery — lions hunting and crouching (vv. 11-12) — rather than more general language about wicked people?
   → The imagery conveys the real, visceral terror of being actively and skillfully hunted, making the appeal for divine rescue feel urgent and concrete rather than abstract, and justifying the intensity of the plea that follows.

4. Why does the psalmist specifically contrast his enemies' apparent prosperity — storing up "wealth for their children" (v. 14) — with his own anticipated satisfaction in seeing God's face (v. 15)?
   → It reframes what counts as ultimate reward; rather than envying the wicked's material success, which is confined to "this life," the psalmist locates his own deepest satisfaction in relationship with God that extends beyond present circumstances and even beyond death.

5. What does the phrase "when I awake, I will be satisfied with seeing your likeness" (v. 15) suggest about the psalmist's confidence even in the midst of present danger?
   → It shows a hope that transcends the immediate crisis — whether referring to waking safely the next morning or to a fuller hope of resurrection, the psalmist's ultimate confidence rests not in the resolution of his immediate circumstances but in an enduring relationship with God that outlasts any present threat.

## Application

- Examine my own life honestly before God, inviting his genuine scrutiny rather than only presenting a polished or defensive version of myself in prayer.
- When facing real threats or opposition, bring vivid, specific language to my prayers rather than only vague requests, naming clearly what I am facing and asking boldly for God's protection.
- Resist envying the apparent prosperity or security of those whose values and priorities are entirely worldly, and instead locate my own deepest satisfaction in relationship with God rather than material accumulation.
- Let hope in ultimately seeing God's face sustain my confidence through present danger or uncertainty, trusting an outcome that extends beyond my immediate circumstances.

## Prayer

Lord, examine my heart honestly, and let me bring you my genuine self rather than a polished or defensive version in prayer. When I face real opposition or threat, give me the boldness to name it clearly and ask directly for your protection, trusting you as my shelter and my shield. Keep me from envying the apparent security of those whose treasure is only in this life, and let my deepest satisfaction be found in seeing your face. Sustain my confidence through whatever I face now with the sure hope of ultimately being with you. Amen.
