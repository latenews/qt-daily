## Text (NIV)

**15** When Gideon heard the dream and its interpretation, he worshiped God. He returned to the camp of Israel and called out, "Get up! The LORD has given the Midianite camp into your hands."
**16** Dividing the three hundred men into three companies, he placed trumpets and empty jars in the hands of all of them, with torches inside.
**17** "Watch me," he told them. "Follow my lead. When I get to the edge of the camp, do exactly as I do.
**18** When I and all who are with me blow our trumpets, then from all around the camp blow yours and shout, 'For the LORD and for Gideon.'"
**19** Gideon and the hundred men with him reached the edge of the camp at the beginning of the middle watch, just after they had changed the guard. They blew their trumpets and broke the jars that were in their hands.
**20** The three companies blew the trumpets and smashed the jars. Grasping the torches in their left hands and holding in their right hands the trumpets they were to blow, they shouted, "A sword for the LORD and for Gideon!"
**21** While each man held his position around the camp, all the Midianites ran, crying out as they fled.
**22** When the three hundred trumpets sounded, the LORD caused the men throughout the camp to turn on each other with their swords. The army fled to Beth Shittah toward Zererah as far as the border of Abel Meholah near Tabbath.
**23** Israelites from Naphtali, Asher and all Manasseh were called out, and they pursued the Midianites.
**24** Gideon sent messengers throughout the hill country of Ephraim, saying, "Come down against the Midianites and seize the waters of the Jordan ahead of them as far as Beth Barah." So all the men of Ephraim were called out and they took the waters of the Jordan as far as Beth Barah.
**25** They also captured two of the Midianite leaders, Oreb and Zeeb. They killed Oreb at the rock of Oreb, and Zeeb at the winepress of Zeeb. They pursued the Midianites and brought the heads of Oreb and Zeeb to Gideon, who was by the Jordan.

## Observation

**Structure (3 parts)**
- Verse 15: Gideon's worship and immediate call to action after hearing the dream's interpretation
- Verses 16-22: The unusual battle plan — trumpets, jars, torches — and its stunning success as the enemy turns on itself
- Verses 23-25: The wider mobilization of Israel's tribes and the capture of the Midianite leaders

**Key repeated words/phrases**
- "For the LORD and for Gideon" (v. 18, 20) — the battle cry names the LORD first, keeping the true source of victory explicit even in the heat of the moment
- "trumpets," "jars," "torches" (repeated across vv. 16, 19, 20) — an unconventional, almost theatrical set of "weapons" repeated with careful detail
- "fled" / "pursued" (v. 21-23, 25) — the rapid shift from the enemy's flight to Israel's pursuit shows the completeness of the reversal

**Contrast structure**
- Three hundred men holding trumpets, jars, and torches (v. 16) — not a single sword mentioned among the three hundred — vs. "the LORD caused the men throughout the camp to turn on each other with their swords" (v. 22) — the enemy's own swords, not Israel's, become the instrument of destruction
- Gideon's earlier admitted fear (7:10) vs. his immediate, confident action after hearing the dream ("Get up!" v. 15) — encouragement transforms hesitation into decisive leadership within moments
- A small, initial band of three hundred (v. 16) vs. the eventual mobilization of Naphtali, Asher, Manasseh, and Ephraim (vv. 23-24) — the LORD's initial small victory expands into a much larger, coordinated national response

**Notable expressions**
- v. 15 "he worshiped God" — before giving a single order, Gideon's first response to encouragement is worship, not merely strategy or excitement
- v. 20 "torches in their left hands... trumpets... in their right hands" — the specific physical detail underscores that every man's hands were full of noise and light, none free to hold a weapon, making the ensuing chaos entirely the LORD's doing
- v. 22 "turn on each other with their swords" — the enemy's own confusion and self-destruction, not Israelite swordsmanship, accomplishes the great majority of the slaughter

## Interpretation (Questions)

1. Why does Gideon respond to the encouraging dream by worshiping God first (v. 15), before immediately organizing his men for battle?
   → It models the right order of response to encouragement from God — gratitude and worship precede action, keeping the victory rightly oriented toward its true source before any strategy is executed.

2. Why does the LORD's battle plan involve trumpets, jars, and torches instead of any actual weapons for the three hundred men?
   → It ensures, in the most literal and visible way possible, that Israel's three hundred did not defeat Midian by force of arms; the confusion, flight, and mutual slaughter within the Midianite camp is unmistakably the LORD's own doing, using noise and light rather than swords.

3. What is the significance of the battle cry "For the LORD and for Gideon" naming both the LORD and Gideon together?
   → It reflects a properly ordered partnership between divine initiative and human leadership — Gideon truly leads and is credited, yet always in a way explicitly subordinated to and dependent on the LORD who is named first.

4. Why does the passage detail the specific way each man held his torch and trumpet (v. 20), down to which hand held what?
   → The specificity emphasizes that literally no hand was free to hold a weapon, reinforcing without ambiguity that the resulting chaos and victory could not have come from the three hundred men's fighting ability.

5. What does the escalating mobilization of additional tribes (vv. 23-24) after the initial three-hundred-man victory suggest about how God's work often unfolds?
   → A small, seemingly inadequate act of obedience (three hundred men with jars and torches) becomes the trigger for a much larger movement of deliverance involving many more people — God's initial, hidden work often expands into visible, widespread impact through others responding to what he has already begun.

## Application

- When encouragement or good news comes from God, let my first response be worship and gratitude before rushing into planning or action.
- Trust that God can accomplish far more through methods that leave no room for me to claim credit (trumpets and jars rather than swords) than through impressive shows of my own strength or ability.
- Remember that the credit for any victory in my life belongs first to the LORD, even when I have genuinely worked and led, as Gideon did — keep the order of the "battle cry" right.
- Do not despise small acts of obedience; the three hundred men's initial action is exactly what triggered the larger movement of deliverance that followed.
