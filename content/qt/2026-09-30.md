## Text (NIV)

**1** Samson went down to Timnah and saw there a young Philistine woman.
**2** When he returned, he said to his father and mother, "I have seen a Philistine woman in Timnah; now get her for me as my wife."
**3** His father and mother replied, "Isn't there an acceptable woman among your relatives or among all our people? Must you go to the uncircumcised Philistines to get a wife?" But Samson said to his father, "Get her for me. She's the right one for me."
**4** (His parents did not know that this was from the LORD, who was seeking an occasion to confront the Philistines; for at that time they were ruling over Israel.)
**5** Samson went down to Timnah together with his father and mother. As they approached the vineyards of Timnah, suddenly a young lion came roaring toward him.
**6** The Spirit of the LORD came upon him in power so that he tore the lion apart with his bare hands as he might have torn a young goat. But he told neither his father nor his mother what he had done.
**7** Then he went down and talked with the woman, and he liked her.
**8** Some time later, when he went back to marry her, he turned aside to look at the lion's carcass. In it was a swarm of bees and some honey,
**9** which he scooped out with his hands and ate as he went along. When he rejoined his parents, he gave them some, and they too ate it. But he did not tell them that he had taken the honey from the lion's carcass.
**10** Now his father went down to see the woman. And Samson made a feast there, as was customary for bridegrooms.
**11** When he appeared, he was given thirty companions.
**12** "Let me tell you a riddle," Samson said to them. "If you can give me the answer within the seven days of the feast, I will give you thirty linen garments and thirty sets of clothes.
**13** If you can't tell me the answer, you must give me thirty linen garments and thirty sets of clothes." "Tell us your riddle," they said. "Let's hear it."
**14** He replied, "Out of the eater, something to eat; out of the strong, something sweet." For three days they could not give the answer.
**15** On the fourth day, they said to Samson's wife, "Coax your husband into explaining the riddle for us, or we will burn you and your father's household to death. Did you invite us here to rob us?"
**16** Then Samson's wife threw herself on him, sobbing, "You hate me! You don't really love me. You've given my people a riddle, but you haven't told me the answer." "I haven't even explained it to my father or mother," he replied, "so why should I explain it to you?"
**17** She cried the whole seven days of the feast. So on the seventh day he finally told her, because she continued to press him. She in turn explained the riddle to her people.
**18** Before sunset on the seventh day the men of the town said to him, "What is sweeter than honey? What is stronger than a lion?" Samson said to them, "If you had not plowed with my heifer, you would not have solved my riddle."
**19** Then the Spirit of the LORD came upon him in power. He went down to Ashkelon, struck down thirty of their men, stripped them of their belongings and gave their clothes to those who had explained the riddle. Burning with anger, he went up to his father's house.
**20** And Samson's wife was given to the friend who had attended him at his wedding.

## Observation

**Structure (3 parts)**
- Verses 1-4: Samson's demand to marry a Philistine woman against his parents' objection, with the narrator's aside about God's hidden purpose
- Verses 5-9: The lion encounter, the Spirit's empowerment, and Samson's private, secretive relationship to what happened
- Verses 10-20: The riddle contest, its manipulation through his wife, and the violent, angry resolution

**Key repeated words/phrases**
- "did not know" / "did not tell" (v. 4, 6, 9) — repeated across the chapter, showing how much of God's purpose and Samson's own actions remain hidden from others, including his own parents
- "the Spirit of the LORD came upon him in power" (v. 6, 19) — the same empowering phrase brackets both the lion-killing and the vengeful killing of thirty men, showing continuity of divine empowerment even amid Samson's flawed motives
- "she's the right one for me" / "he liked her" (v. 3, 7) — Samson's personal desire, stated plainly and without deeper reflection, drives the narrative's initial and ongoing direction

**Contrast structure**
- The parents' reasonable objection, rooted in covenant faithfulness ("must you go to the uncircumcised Philistines," v. 3) vs. the narrator's aside that this very relationship was "from the LORD" for a hidden purpose (v. 4) — human wisdom and divine sovereignty operate on different, sometimes surprising, levels within the same event
- Samson's supernatural strength (tearing a lion apart bare-handed, v. 6) vs. his very ordinary, almost petty vulnerability to his wife's tears and pressure (vv. 16-17) — immense physical power coexists with significant emotional and relational susceptibility
- The riddle framed as playful entertainment with a wager (vv. 12-14) vs. its resolution through threat, betrayal, and mass killing (vv. 15, 19) — what begins as a feast game ends in violence and broken trust
- Samson's private silence about the lion and honey (v. 6, 9) vs. his wife's public disclosure of his riddle's answer under threat (v. 17) — secrecy kept out of personal restraint contrasts with secrecy broken under coercion

**Notable expressions**
- v. 4 "seeking an occasion to confront the Philistines" — a rare narratorial insight revealing that God can work his purposes through and even despite deeply flawed human choices and relationships
- v. 18 "if you had not plowed with my heifer" — a crude, agricultural metaphor exposing Samson's anger at being manipulated through his wife, revealing both his wit and his volatility
- v. 20 Samson's wife given to another man — an abrupt, almost administrative note ending the chapter, showing the human cost of the entire episode largely unaddressed by Samson himself

## Interpretation (Questions)

1. What does it mean that God was "seeking an occasion to confront the Philistines" (v. 4) through Samson's ill-advised, self-willed marriage decision, which his own parents rightly questioned?
   → It reveals that God's sovereign purposes can work even through human choices that are not themselves wise or faithful; this does not excuse or endorse Samson's decision, but shows that God's larger plan is not thwarted by human folly, even while human responsibility for that folly remains real.

2. Why does Samson keep both the lion-killing (v. 6) and the honey (v. 9) secret from his parents, and what might this reveal about his character?
   → It shows a pattern of self-contained secrecy and independence, perhaps even carelessness about ritual boundaries (a Nazirite touching a carcass), suggesting Samson increasingly operates by his own private impulses rather than in full transparency or accountability to his family or his calling.

3. Why is Samson, despite his immense physical strength, so easily manipulated by his wife's tears and pressure (vv. 16-17), just as he will be later by Delilah?
   → It exposes a recurring vulnerability in his character — physical strength granted by the Spirit does not translate into emotional discipline or wisdom in relationships, showing that giftedness in one area does not guarantee maturity in every area of life.

4. Why does the riddle contest escalate so quickly from playful wager to violent threat and mass killing (vv. 15-19)?
   → It illustrates how quickly seemingly minor social conflicts, especially those involving pride, deception, and cross-cultural tension, can spiral into serious violence, particularly in a context already marked by hostility between Israel and the Philistines.

5. What does the chapter's abrupt ending (v. 20), in which Samson's wife is simply given to another man, suggest about how this narrative treats the human relationships damaged along the way?
   → It shows the ongoing human cost of Samson's choices treated almost as an afterthought within the larger narrative, inviting the reader to notice and feel the weight of what the main character himself seems to overlook.

## Application

- Recognize that God's purposes can still move forward even through my own or others' unwise decisions, without treating that as license to make unwise decisions on the assumption that God will simply work around them.
- Consider whether there are areas of my life kept in careless secrecy, not out of humility but out of a habit of operating independently without accountability, as Samson did with the lion and the honey.
- Notice where a particular strength or gifting in my life might coexist with an unaddressed vulnerability in another area, especially in relationships, and seek maturity in both rather than assuming strength in one area covers weakness in another.
- Pay attention to the relational and human cost of my own conflicts or decisions, especially those I might otherwise treat as minor disputes or games, before they escalate into deeper harm.

## Prayer
