## Text (NIV)

**1** Again the Israelites did evil in the eyes of the LORD, so the LORD delivered them into the hands of the Philistines for forty years.
**2** A certain man of Zorah, named Manoah, from the clan of the Danites, had a wife who was sterile and remained childless.
**3** The angel of the LORD appeared to her and said, "You are sterile and childless, but you are going to conceive and have a son.
**4** Now see to it that you drink no wine or other fermented drink and that you do not eat anything unclean,
**5** because you will conceive and give birth to a son. No razor may be used on his head, because the boy is to be a Nazirite, set apart to God from birth, and he will begin the deliverance of Israel from the hands of the Philistines."
**6** Then the woman went to her husband and told him, "A man of God came to me. He looked like an angel of God, very awesome. I didn't ask him where he came from, and he didn't tell me his name.
**7** But he said to me, 'You will conceive and give birth to a son. Now then, drink no wine or other fermented drink and do not eat anything unclean, because the boy will be a Nazirite of God from birth until the day of his death.'"
**8** Then Manoah prayed to the LORD: "O Lord, I beg you, let the man of God you sent to us come again to teach us how to bring up the boy who is to be born."
**9** God heard Manoah, and the angel of God came again to the woman while she was out in the field; but her husband Manoah was not with her.
**10** The woman hurried to tell her husband, "He's here! The man who appeared to me the other day!"
**11** Manoah got up and followed his wife. When he came to the man, he said, "Are you the one who talked to my wife?" "I am," he said.
**12** So Manoah asked him, "When your words are fulfilled, what is to be the rule for the boy's life and work?"
**13** The angel of the LORD answered, "Your wife must do all that I have told her.
**14** She must not eat anything that comes from the grapevine, nor drink any wine or other fermented drink nor eat anything unclean. She must do everything I have commanded her."

## Observation

**Structure (2 parts)**
- Verses 1-7: The cycle begins again with Philistine oppression, and the angel of the LORD's first appearance is given only to Manoah's wife
- Verses 8-14: Manoah's prayer for a second visit, and the angel's return with a repeated, unchanged instruction

**Key repeated words/phrases**
- "sterile and childless" (v. 2, 3) — repeated within two verses, immediately establishing the depth of the couple's need before any promise is given
- "wine or other fermented drink... anything unclean" (v. 4, 7, 14) — the Nazirite dietary instruction is repeated nearly word for word three times, emphasizing its unwavering importance
- "the angel of the LORD" / "a man of God" (used interchangeably throughout) — the identity of the messenger shifts in the human characters' description even while the narrator consistently names him precisely

**Contrast structure**
- No cry for help from Israel is recorded in this cycle (contrast with previous cycles' "cried out to the LORD") vs. God's initiative in sending the angel anyway — grace arrives here without any narrated repentance or petition preceding it
- The angel's first appearance to the wife alone (v. 3) vs. Manoah's active pursuit of a second appearance for himself (vv. 8-11) — the narrative highlights differing responses and needs between the two spouses regarding the same revelation
- Manoah's specific question about "the rule for the boy's life and work" (v. 12) vs. the angel's answer, which addresses only the mother's diet and behavior, not the child's future actions directly — Manoah's forward-looking question is met with an answer rooted in the present, ordinary obedience required of his wife

**Notable expressions**
- v. 1 no cry for help recorded — unusual within the cycle pattern of Judges, where oppression is typically followed explicitly by Israel's cry to the LORD before a deliverer is announced; here the announcement of a deliverer (Samson) comes seemingly unprompted by repentance
- v. 6 "he looked like an angel of God, very awesome" — the wife's own description captures both the messenger's overwhelming presence and her own uncertainty about his exact identity
- v. 12 "what is to be the rule for the boy's life and work" — Manoah's eager, detailed question about the child's future reveals a father's care and anticipation even before the child is born

## Interpretation (Questions)

1. Why does this cycle of the book of Judges begin without any recorded cry from Israel for deliverance, unlike the pattern established in earlier chapters?
   → It shows that the LORD's initiative in raising up deliverance is not strictly dependent on Israel's repentance being narrated each time; God's grace and providence can move ahead of, or even without waiting for, a formal cry for help, foreshadowing his sovereign freedom to act.

2. Why does the angel appear first to Manoah's wife alone, and what significance might there be in the specific attention paid to her within the narrative?
   → It highlights her role and responsibility as central to the child's future consecration (the dietary restrictions rest specifically on her during pregnancy), giving her an active, essential place in the unfolding story rather than treating her as incidental to her husband's account.

3. Why does Manoah specifically ask for the angel to return and later ask about "the rule for the boy's life and work" (v. 12), even though the essential message has already been fully delivered to his wife?
   → It reflects a father's earnest desire to be personally involved and prepared, not merely informed secondhand; his request models an eagerness to receive guidance directly rather than relying solely on another's report, even a trustworthy one.

4. Why does the angel's answer to Manoah's forward-looking question about the boy's "work" focus entirely on the mother's present, ordinary obedience (v. 13-14) rather than addressing the child's future actions?
   → It suggests that faithful preparation for a significant calling begins with unglamorous, present obedience (what the mother eats and avoids) rather than grand plans about future accomplishments — the child's eventual "work" is rooted in disciplined, ordinary faithfulness from before birth.

5. What does the repeated emphasis on strict, unchanging dietary and vow requirements (repeated three times across the passage) suggest about the seriousness of the child's set-apart status?
   → The consistency and repetition underscore that Samson's Nazirite consecration was to be taken with utmost seriousness from before birth, setting up significant tension and tragedy later when Samson himself repeatedly disregards the very boundaries established here for his sake.

## Application

- Trust that God's purposes and initiative in my life are not strictly limited to responding only after I have recognized my need and cried out; he may already be at work preparing something before I have fully sought him.
- Value the unglamorous, present, ordinary obedience required of me now as foundational to whatever future calling or purpose God has in mind, rather than focusing only on future "work" or accomplishment.
- Follow Manoah's example of actively seeking direct guidance and involvement rather than being satisfied with only secondhand information when God's purposes concern my own family or responsibilities.
- Take seriously any specific boundaries or disciplines God has called me to maintain, recognizing that consistency in seemingly small matters often prepares the ground for something significant later.
