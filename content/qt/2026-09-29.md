## Text (NIV)

**15** Manoah said to the angel of the LORD, "We would like you to stay until we prepare a young goat for you."
**16** The angel of the LORD replied, "Even though you detain me, I will not eat any of your food. But if you prepare a burnt offering, offer it to the LORD." (Manoah did not realize that it was the angel of the LORD.)
**17** Then Manoah inquired of the angel of the LORD, "What is your name, so that we may honor you when your word comes true?"
**18** He replied, "Why do you ask my name? It is beyond understanding."
**19** Then Manoah took a young goat, together with the grain offering, and sacrificed it on a rock to the LORD. And the LORD did an amazing thing while Manoah and his wife watched:
**20** As the flame blazed up from the altar toward heaven, the angel of the LORD ascended in the flame. Seeing this, Manoah and his wife fell with their faces to the ground.
**21** When the angel of the LORD did not show himself again to Manoah and his wife, Manoah realized that it was the angel of the LORD.
**22** "We are doomed to die!" he said to his wife. "We have seen God!"
**23** But his wife answered, "If the LORD had meant to kill us, he would not have accepted a burnt offering and grain offering from our hands, nor shown us all these things or now told us this."
**24** The woman gave birth to a boy and named him Samson. He grew and the LORD blessed him,
**25** and the Spirit of the LORD began to stir him while he was in Mahaneh Dan, between Zorah and Eshtaol.

## Observation

**Structure (2 parts)**
- Verses 15-21: Manoah's hospitality, his question about the angel's name, and the dramatic ascension in the altar's flame
- Verses 22-25: The couple's contrasting reactions to what they have witnessed, followed by Samson's birth and early growth

**Key repeated words/phrases**
- "the angel of the LORD" (v. 15, 16, 17, 20, 21) — repeated insistently by the narrator even while Manoah himself remains unaware of the messenger's true identity until later, creating dramatic irony
- "did not realize" / "realized" (v. 16, 21) — this pair of phrases frames the entire episode as a process of gradual recognition rather than immediate understanding
- "we have seen God" / "if the LORD had meant to kill us" (v. 22-23) — the couple's dialogue directly answers Manoah's fear with his wife's calm, logical reassurance

**Contrast structure**
- Manoah's fearful assumption, "we are doomed to die... we have seen God" (v. 22) vs. his wife's composed, evidence-based reasoning (v. 23) — the narrative gives the more clear-headed theological insight to the wife rather than the husband, echoing the pattern already seen with Deborah and Barak
- The angel's refusal to reveal his name ("it is beyond understanding," v. 18) vs. the plain, visible demonstration of his identity and power in the ascending flame (v. 20) — some mysteries are not explained in words but are nonetheless made unmistakably clear through action
- Human doubt and fear (v. 22) vs. divine blessing and quiet growth immediately following (vv. 24-25) — the fearful uncertainty of the parents gives way to the peaceful, ordinary description of a child growing under God's blessing

**Notable expressions**
- v. 18 "why do you ask my name? It is beyond understanding" — the angel's answer resists simple explanation, suggesting that some aspects of God's nature and messengers exceed full human comprehension even when directly revealed
- v. 20 "the angel of the LORD ascended in the flame" — a vivid, unmistakable sign accompanying the sacrifice, leaving no room for doubt about who had actually been speaking with them
- v. 23 the wife's reasoning is calm, practical, and theologically sound — using the very evidence of God's gracious acceptance of their offering and revelation as proof against her husband's fear of judgment

## Interpretation (Questions)

1. Why does Manoah remain unaware of the angel's true identity for so long (v. 16), even after receiving such an extraordinary message twice?
   → It reflects how divine encounters are not always immediately or fully understood even by sincere, faithful people; recognition can be a gradual process, and full clarity sometimes comes only after reflecting on the totality of the evidence.

2. Why does the angel refuse to give his name, saying it is "beyond understanding" (v. 18), rather than simply answering Manoah's reasonable request?
   → It preserves a sense of holy mystery about God's messenger, suggesting that not every legitimate question deserves or requires a direct verbal answer — some realities about God are better shown through action (the ascending flame) than explained through a name or category.

3. Why is Manoah's wife given the theologically clearer, more composed response to their shared fear (v. 23), rather than Manoah himself?
   → It continues a pattern in Judges of unexpected sources of clarity and faith — women in this book (Deborah, Jael, and now Manoah's wife) repeatedly demonstrate sound judgment and courage, often surpassing the men around them in critical moments.

4. What is the logical strength of the wife's argument in verse 23, and why is it persuasive enough to resolve Manoah's fear?
   → Her reasoning works backward from God's own demonstrated actions (accepting their offering, revealing these things, speaking further promises) to conclude that a God intent on their destruction would not have done any of this — sound theological reflection, not mere optimism, calms the fear.

5. Why does the passage move so quickly and calmly from the couple's terror (v. 22) to Samson's peaceful growth "blessed" by the LORD (vv. 24-25)?
   → It shows that intense fear or uncertainty in the moment of encountering God does not have to define what follows; genuine encounters with God, even overwhelming ones, are meant to lead toward blessing and ordinary faithful growth rather than remaining fixed in dread.

## Application

- Recognize that not fully understanding a spiritual encounter or answer right away does not mean it lacks meaning; clarity sometimes comes gradually as I reflect on the fuller picture of what God has done.
- When facing fear rooted in a sense of my own unworthiness before God, practice the same kind of reasoning Manoah's wife used — looking at what God has already shown or given as evidence of his good intentions, rather than assuming the worst.
- Stay open to receiving wisdom and clarity from unexpected sources, including those I might not expect to have the clearer spiritual insight in a given moment.
- Let genuine encounters with God's presence or work in my life move me toward ongoing faithful growth and blessing, rather than remaining stuck in the fear or awe of the moment itself.
