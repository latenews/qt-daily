## Text (NIV)

**11** The angel of the LORD came and sat down under the oak in Ophrah that belonged to Joash the Abiezrite, where his son Gideon was threshing wheat in a winepress to keep it from the Midianites.
**12** When the angel of the LORD appeared to Gideon, he said, "The LORD is with you, mighty warrior."
**13** "But sir," Gideon replied, "if the LORD is with us, why has all this happened to us? Where are all his wonders that our fathers told us about when they said, 'Did not the LORD bring us up out of Egypt?' But now the LORD has abandoned us and put us into the hand of Midian."
**14** The LORD turned to him and said, "Go in the strength you have and save Israel out of Midian's hand. Am I not sending you?"
**15** "But Lord," Gideon asked, "how can I save Israel? My clan is the weakest in Manasseh, and I am the least in my family."
**16** The LORD answered, "I will be with you, and you will strike down all the Midianites together."
**17** Gideon replied, "If now I have found favor in your eyes, give me a sign that it is really you talking to me.
**18** Please do not go away until I come back and bring my offering and set it before you." And the LORD said, "I will wait until you return."
**19** Gideon went in, prepared a young goat, and from an ephah of flour he made bread without yeast. Putting the meat in a basket and its broth in a pot, he brought them out and offered them to him under the oak.
**20** The angel of God said to him, "Take the meat and the unleavened bread, place them on this rock, and pour out the broth." And Gideon did so.
**21** With the tip of the staff that was in his hand, the angel of the LORD touched the meat and the unleavened bread. Fire flared from the rock, consuming the meat and the bread. And the angel of the LORD disappeared.
**22** When Gideon realized that it was the angel of the LORD, he exclaimed, "Ah, Sovereign LORD! I have seen the angel of the LORD face to face!"
**23** But the LORD said to him, "Peace! Do not be afraid. You are not going to die."
**24** So Gideon built an altar to the LORD there and called it The LORD is Peace. To this day it stands in Ophrah of the Abiezrites.

## Observation

**Structure (2 parts)**
- Verses 11-16: The angel of the LORD's call to Gideon, met with doubt, questions, and self-deprecation
- Verses 17-24: Gideon's request for a sign, the fire that confirms it, and his fearful yet worshipful response

**Key repeated words/phrases**
- "mighty warrior" (v. 12) vs. Gideon's own self-description "the least in my family" (v. 15) — a direct contradiction between how the LORD addresses him and how he sees himself
- "Am I not sending you?" (v. 14) — a rhetorical question that answers Gideon's doubt before he even fully voices it
- "Peace! Do not be afraid" (v. 23) — the LORD's own reassurance echoes the same words later given to Gideon's descendant, showing this as a recurring pattern of divine comfort at moments of holy fear

**Contrast structure**
- Gideon hiding to thresh wheat "in a winepress to keep it from the Midianites" (v. 11) vs. being called "mighty warrior" (v. 12) — the LORD names him according to his future calling, not his present fearful posture
- "the LORD has abandoned us" (v. 13, Gideon's complaint) vs. "I will be with you" (v. 16, the LORD's promise) — Gideon's felt experience of abandonment is met directly with a personal promise of presence
- Fire consuming the offering instantly (v. 21) vs. Gideon's terrified assumption that seeing God's messenger means death (v. 22) — divine power provokes fear, but the LORD's actual purpose is peace, not judgment

**Notable expressions**
- v. 11 "threshing wheat in a winepress" — an odd, secretive activity (normally done in the open on a threshing floor) that visually captures Israel's fear and reduced circumstances under Midianite oppression
- v. 13 "if the LORD is with us, why has all this happened" — an honest, almost accusatory question that the text does not rebuke, showing that doubt voiced to God is treated differently than doubt that refuses to engage him at all
- v. 24 "called it The LORD is Peace" — Gideon commemorates the encounter permanently, turning a moment of personal fear into a lasting witness for others

## Interpretation (Questions)

1. Why does the angel address Gideon as "mighty warrior" when he is found hiding and threshing wheat in a winepress out of fear?
   → The LORD often names people according to what he intends to make of them, not merely according to their present state — the calling itself carries the power to become true, since it is grounded in the LORD's presence and purpose, not in Gideon's current courage.

2. Why is Gideon's doubting question in verse 13 not met with rebuke, but instead with a direct commissioning in verse 14?
   → It shows that honest wrestling with God about suffering and his apparent absence is not itself sin; the LORD engages the real question rather than shutting it down, and moves Gideon toward action and trust rather than toward guilt.

3. Why does Gideon feel the need for a sign (v. 17) even after already hearing a direct promise from the LORD in verse 16?
   → It reflects the ordinary human need for confirmation when facing something as costly as fighting an oppressive empire; the LORD's patience in waiting for Gideon to bring the offering (v. 18) shows he is not offended by the request for reassurance.

4. Why does Gideon assume he is going to die simply because he has seen the angel of the LORD face to face (v. 22)?
   → It reflects the ancient conviction that direct, unmediated encounter with the holy God is dangerous for sinful humanity; the LORD's response ("Peace! Do not be afraid," v. 23) reveals that this particular encounter is one of grace and commissioning, not judgment.

5. What is the significance of Gideon naming the place "The LORD is Peace" rather than simply moving on after the fear passed?
   → Naming and building an altar turns a private, fearful moment into a permanent, communal testimony ("to this day it stands"), showing the importance of marking and remembering specific encounters with God rather than letting them fade into forgetfulness.

## Application

- When I feel weak, hidden, or overwhelmed like Gideon threshing in the winepress, remember that God may be calling me toward exactly what I feel least equipped for, and that his naming of me matters more than my self-assessment.
- Bring my honest doubts and hard questions to God directly, as Gideon did, rather than either suppressing them or letting them keep me from responding to his call.
- Recognize that asking for confirmation or reassurance in a costly calling is not necessarily unbelief; ask God for the clarity I need while still remaining willing to obey.
- Consider marking significant moments where God has met me in fear and given peace — through a journal entry, a specific memory, or a marker of some kind — so I do not forget them.
