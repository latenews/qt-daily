## Text (NIV)

**1** Early in the morning, Jerub-Baal (that is, Gideon) and all his men camped at the spring of Harod. The camp of Midian was north of them in the valley near the hill of Moreh.
**2** The LORD said to Gideon, "You have too many men for me to deliver Midian into their hands. In order that Israel may not boast against me that her own strength has saved her,
**3** announce now to the people, 'Anyone who trembles with fear may turn back and leave Mount Gilead.'" So twenty-two thousand men left, while ten thousand remained.
**4** But the LORD said to Gideon, "There are still too many men. Take them down to the water, and I will sift them for you there. If I say, 'This one shall go with you,' he shall go; but if I say, 'This one shall not go with you,' he shall not go."
**5** So Gideon took the men down to the water. There the LORD told him, "Separate those who lap the water with their tongues like a dog from those who kneel down to drink."
**6** Three hundred men lapped with their hands to their mouths. All the rest got down on their knees to drink.
**7** The LORD said to Gideon, "With the three hundred men that lapped I will save you and give the Midianites into your hands. Let all the other men go, each to his own place."
**8** So Gideon sent the rest of the Israelites to their tents but kept the three hundred, who took over the provisions and trumpets of the others. Now the camp of Midian lay below him in the valley.
**9** During that night the LORD said to Gideon, "Get up, go down against the camp, because I am going to give it into your hands.
**10** If you are afraid to attack, go down to the camp with your servant Purah
**11** and listen to what they are saying. Afterward, you will be encouraged to attack the camp." So he and Purah his servant went down to the outposts of the camp.
**12** The Midianites, the Amalekites and all the other eastern peoples had settled in the valley, thick as locusts. Their camels could no more be counted than the sand on the seashore.
**13** Gideon arrived just as a man was telling a friend his dream. "I had a dream," he was saying. "A round loaf of barley bread came tumbling into the Midianite camp. It struck the tent with such force that the tent overturned and collapsed."
**14** His friend responded, "This can be nothing other than the sword of Gideon son of Joash, the Israelite. God has given the Midianites and the whole camp into his hands."

## Observation

**Structure (3 parts)**
- Verses 1-3: The first reduction of Gideon's army, based on fear
- Verses 4-8: The second, stranger reduction, based on how the men drank water
- Verses 9-14: The LORD's provision of encouragement through an overheard dream, offered even before the attack

**Key repeated words/phrases**
- "too many men" (v. 2, 4) — repeated twice, making explicit and undeniable the LORD's reason for shrinking the army
- "give... into your hands" (v. 2, 7, 9, 14) — repeated across nearly every stage, insisting that the coming victory belongs to the LORD before a single blow is struck
- "afraid" (v. 3, 10) — named twice, first regarding the fearful soldiers who leave, then regarding Gideon himself, whom the LORD accommodates rather than shames

**Contrast structure**
- Thirty-two thousand men (implied from twenty-two thousand who left plus ten thousand who remained) reduced to three hundred (v. 3-7) — an almost absurd numerical collapse that makes any coming victory unmistakably attributable to God rather than manpower
- Countless enemy forces "thick as locusts... could no more be counted than the sand on the seashore" (v. 12) vs. Israel's mere three hundred — an overwhelming imbalance deliberately engineered by the LORD's own reductions
- Gideon's private fear, accommodated by God's permission to go listen at the camp (v. 10) vs. the confidence-building word he actually receives there (v. 13-14) — God meets fear not by ignoring it but by providing exactly the encouragement needed

**Notable expressions**
- v. 2 "so that Israel may not boast" — states plainly and directly the theological purpose behind the entire reduction of the army, a purpose statement rare in its explicitness
- v. 5-6 "lapped... like a dog" — an odd, seemingly arbitrary test that nonetheless becomes the LORD's chosen method of selection, showing his freedom to choose by means that appear inexplicable to human logic
- v. 13-14 an enemy soldier's dream and his friend's interpretation — remarkably, the encouragement Gideon needed comes not from a fellow Israelite but from the mouth of the enemy camp itself, a striking irony

## Interpretation (Questions)

1. Why does the LORD insist on reducing Gideon's army not once but twice, using two very different criteria (fear, then the drinking test)?
   → The point is repeated and reinforced: whatever victory occurs must be unmistakably credited to the LORD, not to Israel's own numbers or self-selected courage. The reduction methods seem almost designed to remove any human claim to the outcome.

2. What is the significance of the seemingly arbitrary drinking test (lapping versus kneeling) as the final method of selection?
   → Its very arbitrariness underscores that the LORD's ways of choosing are not bound to human criteria of competence or strategy; he retains sovereign freedom over the means, even when they appear strange or inexplicable to us.

3. Why does the LORD offer Gideon the option to go down and listen at the enemy camp if he is "afraid to attack" (v. 10), rather than simply commanding him to go without concession?
   → It shows that the LORD accommodates real fear with real reassurance rather than demanding blind confidence; God is willing to meet people in their fear with tailored evidence and encouragement rather than only issuing commands.

4. What does it mean that Gideon's crucial encouragement before the battle comes from overhearing an enemy soldier's dream and interpretation, rather than from a word given directly to him?
   → It reveals that God can provide exactly the reassurance a person needs through unexpected, even unlikely channels — including the mouths of one's enemies — showing his sovereignty extends over every source of information, not only the expected ones.

5. How does the enormous disparity between Israel's three hundred and the Midianite horde "thick as locusts" set up the theological point of the coming battle?
   → It ensures that whatever happens next can only be explained by divine intervention; the narrative deliberately manufactures a situation impossible by ordinary military logic so that the coming victory testifies unmistakably to the LORD's power.

## Application

- When I am tempted to rely on my own resources, numbers, or strength in a situation, ask whether God might be inviting me to trust him more directly by first stripping away some of what I depend on.
- Do not assume that unusual or seemingly arbitrary ways God works in my life are meaningless; his methods do not have to make sense by ordinary human logic to be his methods.
- Bring my fear honestly to God rather than hiding it, trusting that he may provide the specific encouragement I need, sometimes through unexpected sources.
- Watch for God's provision of encouragement coming from surprising places — a stranger's comment, an unlikely circumstance — and be ready to receive it as from him.

## Prayer

Lord, thank you that your victories are never dependent on my strength, numbers, or resources, and that you are free to work through methods that may not make sense to me. When I feel afraid, like Gideon, meet me with the specific encouragement I need rather than demanding a courage I do not yet have. Help me to trust you even when what you ask me to release feels like it leaves me exposed and outnumbered, and give me eyes to recognize your encouragement when it comes from unexpected places.
